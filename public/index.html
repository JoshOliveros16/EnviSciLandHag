Hagonoy GroundWatch

Hagonoy is sinking

Land subsidence is the slow drop of the ground surface. Pumping groundwater is the largest single cause. In low coastal towns like Hagonoy, Bulacan, a lower ground means more flooding, and squeezed clay does not spring back.

Home: Hagonoy overview

A low town beside the bay

Hagonoy is a coastal town where marsh and river channels meet Manila Bay. Land subsidence lowers the ground, so floods and tides reach further each year.

Hagonoy covers 103.10 sq km at the southwest corner of Bulacan and has 26 barangays. Its land is a long ridge of marsh and tributaries that drain to the sea, so it was low and wet even before the ground started to sink.

The municipal engineer has said the town is sinking by about 10 cm a year on top of its natural flood risk. A 2026 study of Hagonoy and Calumpit linked heavy groundwater extraction and urban growth to subsidence there.

About land subsidence

How pumping drops the ground

The US Geological Survey calls compaction of aquifer systems by far the single largest cause of subsidence. Four steps explain the diagram above.

  1. Step 1

    Water carries load

    Water in the pores of clay and sand pushes back against the weight of the ground above.

  2. Step 2

    Wells pull water out

    Farms, factories and households pump faster than the aquifer refills.

  3. Step 3

    Clay squeezes

    With less water pressure, the grains pack closer. Clay layers lose the most height.

  4. Step 4

    The surface drops

    Roads, pipes and buildings sink with the ground. Compacted clay stays compact. Mexico City's clay is forecast to keep compacting for about 150 years.

Causes

What pulls the ground down

Groundwater pumping leads the list. Four processes account for the sinking USGS tracks.

Groundwater pumping
Aquifer compaction is by far the single largest cause. The pumped water leaves, and the clay above and between the sand layers settles into the space.
Drained peat and wet soils
Drained organic soil decomposes and shrinks. Some wetlands lose 1 to 3 inches (2.5 to 7.5 cm) a year.
Collapsing rock
Water dissolves salt and gypsum underground, and the roof of the cavity falls in. Dropping groundwater levels often trigger the collapse. Rocks of this type sit under 35 to 40 percent of the United States.
Mines, thaw and settling
Underground mining, thawing permafrost, hydrocompaction and natural compaction complete the list.

Causes and effects in Hagonoy

Pumping, flooding, broken structures

Three linked problems. Each one makes the next worse.

Groundwater extraction

Wells serve homes, fish farms and businesses. When pumping outpaces refill, clay layers compact and the surface drops. The ISPRS study ties Hagonoy's extraction volume to measurable sinking.

Flooding

A lower town is a bigger basin. Regular high tides, backwater from Manila Bay and river overflow now cover land that used to stay dry, and some areas stay under water for long periods after typhoons and monsoon rains.

Infrastructure damage

Roads, dikes and foundations settle unevenly and crack. Raising roads alone has been reported to push water into lower neighbouring areas, and several flood control works have been questioned for poor quality.

Effects

What sinking costs

40%

Of Jakarta sits below sea level

The north dropped as much as 4 m in four decades. Pumping and heavy construction drive the sinking, and piped water does not reach everyone, so residents keep drilling wells.

Broken structures

Ground sinks unevenly, so foundations crack, roads buckle and pipes pull apart. Well casings rise out of the surface, as the diagram shows.

Lost storage

Compacted clay holds less water, so the aquifer keeps a smaller volume even after pumping stops.

Community impact

Daily life on sinking ground

The effects reach homes, work, health and even burial grounds.

Livelihood

A farmer interviewed by GMA said land he used to till has turned into swamp. Fishponds and farms near the bay are the most exposed.

Homes and safety

Residents describe chest-high floodwater and living on upper floors. Wading through dirty floodwater raises the risk of skin infections and leptospirosis.

Heritage and burial

Two cemeteries in Barangay San Sebastian were reported to stay submerged by tidal floods nearly all year, and families move remains to higher ground.

Response

What worked in Tokyo

Tokyo sank as much as 4.6 m. The city then restricted groundwater use over two decades and gave industry river water instead.

  1. 1950

    Tokyo starts restricting groundwater use.

  2. 1953

    A Land Subsidence Prevention Council forms.

  3. 1956

    The Law of Groundwater Usage for Industry takes effect.

  4. 1962

    A building water law takes effect and the industrial water law is revised.

  5. 1964

    River water and other alternative sources start reaching industry.

  6. 1970

    The restrictions apply in full and groundwater use stops completely.

Tools for any city

Measure from orbit

Satellite radar (InSAR) maps ground movement at 80 m by 80 m detail with sub-centimeter precision.

License and meter wells

Unregulated wells supply homes, factories and farms near Manila Bay. Permits and meters show who pumps how much.

Pipe in surface water

Tokyo moved industry onto river water by 1964. Jakarta residents still pump because the piped network leaves gaps.

Map before building

Find the fastest-sinking zones before placing roads, rail and housing on them.

Mitigation and solutions

What can be done in Hagonoy

Subsidence is slow and mostly permanent, so action means stopping the cause and living safely with the loss.

Community actions

  • Report cracks, tilting and flooding using the form below.
  • Save water at home and fix leaks.
  • Keep canals and drains free of garbage.
  • Join barangay clean-ups and disaster drills.

Government initiatives

  • Enforce water permits and well metering under the Water Code (NWRB).
  • Build and inspect flood control works, with public checks on quality.
  • Map subsidence with satellite data before approving roads and housing.
  • Expand safe piped water so households need fewer wells.

Sustainable practices

  • Rainwater harvesting for non-drinking use.
  • Protect mangroves and wetlands that slow tides.
  • Build homes on raised, flood-ready foundations.
  • Use surface water instead of deep wells, as Tokyo did.

Interactive feature

Test what you know

Six questions. You get feedback after each answer.

Is Hagonoy sinking because of climate change?

Sea level rise adds to flooding, but subsidence is a separate process driven mainly by groundwater pumping and soil compaction. The two combine.

Can the ground rise again?

Rarely. Compacted clay mostly stays compact, so prevention matters more than repair.

Is it safe to stop using my well?

Only if you have safe piped water. Check with your water provider and barangay before changing your supply.
